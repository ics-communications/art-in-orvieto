How to apply
I  ·  The Town

An advanced summer studies program, ninety minutes north of Rome

ART in Orvieto offers an ecumenical exploration of Christian understandings of the arts: a residency designed for practicing artists, art teachers, graduate students in relevant fields, and other adult learners interested in engaging the intersection of art, religion, and theology.

The program moves at the pace of the town: unhurried days of reading, conversation, and sustained studio work, punctuated by excursions to the art of Rome, Assisi, and Florence.

II  ·  The Monastery

A renovated 13th-century convent, held in common

The program occupies the superbly renovated medieval monastery of the Servite Order in Orvieto, the Convento dei Servi, positioned in the historic hilltop city centre, steps away from the ruins of an Etruscan temple and spectacular views of the valley below, one direction heading toward Florence and the other toward Rome. Accommodations, seminar rooms, and studio space are all housed within its walls.

The monastery is designed around a large cloister, and the whole complex dates to the 1200s. The Servite church, Santa Maria dei Servi, a large architectural gem refashioned in the 1800s, forms the west side of the cloister. The classroom where the Graduate Seminar meets sits across from the church on the main floor and contains a small library for participants' use. The studio runs along the north side of the cloister: two adjoining spaces under a beautiful vaulted ceiling from the 1200s, with plenty of room for sustained work. Each Studio Residency participant is allocated a personal workstation for the duration of the program, with continuous access to the studio throughout.

The space is leased from Gordon College of Wenham, Massachusetts, whose Studio for Art, Faith, and History makes its home here.

III  ·  The Hours

One seminar, one studio: the heart of the program

The program has two components, and participants may take both, or only one. The Seminar and the Studio Residency meet during the week; the studio itself is open to residency participants at all hours, and the library, classroom, and common area are available to everyone throughout the program. Priority for admission is given to those taking both.

The Graduate Seminar

Art, Religion, and Theology: Theologies of Art in the Christian Tradition

led by Rebekah Smick

For some, the idea of an expressly Christian art is a literal impossibility in the contemporary art world context: the sheer notion seems to defy the very nature of art as we understand it, suggesting propaganda rather than the free expression of an individual artist. Yet explicitly Christian art dominates the walls of most Western art museums, and much of it is thought to represent the height of Western art practice. What is this puzzling history able to tell us about the relationship of art to Christianity? How have Christians understood art to function religiously, and what relation does that have to how art is conceived in the modern context?

The seminar examines the art traditions of the three main branches of Christianity (Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant) in their historical contexts, the theology pertinent to their understandings of the religious image, and what contemporary Christian philosophers and theologians have to say about the possibilities for Christian art in modern society. Taking full advantage of the setting, the seminar explores the art of Orvieto and its region as well as of Rome, Assisi, and Florence, in a mix of lecture and discussion on assigned readings.

The Artists' Studio Residency

Sustained work on a particular body of work

led by David Holt

The goal of the studio residency is to help practicing artists make substantial progress with a particular body of work. Participants develop their artworks through various stages, from initial inspirations, ideas, and studies to more fully realized visual forms, while exploring relevant religious, theological, and art historical dimensions. The emphasis falls on the process of creation more than final completion, though there will be opportunities to display work produced during the residency.

Works in a variety of media are encouraged and supported. Practicing artists of all levels of experience are welcome, and guidance is tailored to individual needs: help with materials, techniques, and design issues; assistance in furthering technical skills; and direction in concept development, research, and interpretive methodologies. Alongside ongoing individual consultation, weekly sharing of one's progress with the group provides informed feedback and support. A list of basic supplies is sent to participants upon registration.

The Graduate Credit Option

A formal course of study and practice at the graduate level. Those who complete the graduate requirements in the Seminar and/or Studio Residency may receive graduate credit toward: the Master of Worldview Studies (MWS-ART) degree program of the Institute for Christian Studies; a degree program in the Toronto School of Theology; or a degree program in another institution that accepts transfer credit. Credit is given to students formally admitted to graduate study in the respective institution.

The Audit Option

Regular attendance and full participation in the Seminar and/or Studio Residency, on a not-for-credit basis: completing the readings and joining in discussion; engaging in work on one's own artistic project and sharing in artistic conversation with others. Participants taking this option can receive a Certificate of Completion for studies in ART in Orvieto, suited to those seeking professional development rather than graduate credit.

IV  ·  The People

Your leaders in Orvieto

Rebekah Smick

Graduate Seminar Leader & Program Director

Associate Professor of Philosophy of the Arts and Culture at the Institute for Christian Studies, an affiliate of the Toronto School of Theology. Her publications include a book on the Western survival of classical culture and book chapters on Italian art criticism and theory. She is a contributor to the book accompanying the 2016–17 Art Gallery of Ontario / Musée d'Orsay exhibition Mystical Landscapes: Masterpieces from Monet, Van Gogh and More.

View Rebekah's faculty page

David Holt

Artists' Studio Residency Leader

A graduate of the Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze, David Holt has special interests in Italian art and world religions. He has exhibited widely in the USA and Canada, held a painting grant from the Ludwig Vogelstein Foundation and a residency at the Ragdale Foundation, and was a member of Loop Gallery, an artists' collective in Toronto. A former long-time Professor of Art at Marymount College, Tarrytown (later of Fordham University), he now teaches art at Upper Canada College.

V  ·  The Stay

Rooms in the convent, meals in the refectory

Accommodation

  • Rooms are single or double occupancy, always with single (twin) beds, plus a desk and chair and a private en suite bathroom; towels and bedding provided.
  • Indicate your room preference on the application form; preferences are honoured as room availability at the monastery allows. Single rooms are limited.
  • Air conditioning in some of the convent's public spaces during the summer months; all rooms are provided with fans. Wifi in guest rooms and public spaces.

Meals

  • Continental breakfast, included seven days a week.
  • A larger communal midday meal and evening dinner in the convent's refectory, Monday to Saturday, for the duration of the program. These are included in the program cost, as are meals on excursion Fridays.
  • Weekends are yours: participants make their own arrangements, taking advantage of the wonderful range of restaurants in Orvieto.

What is not included

Travel documents

Entry documentation for Italy is each participant's responsibility and depends on your nationality, residence, and length of stay. Ensure your passport is valid for at least three months beyond your stay, consult the Italian visa requirements for your country, and check the status of the ETIAS travel authorisation, which is in the process of becoming required for travel to EU countries, including Italy.

Cancellation policy

  2. ICS will refund tuition fees (not including registration fees) if the enrollee gives written notice of withdrawal by the published fees deadline; tuition fees are not refundable after that time.

  4. ICS is not liable for personal or travel expenses incurred by enrollees beyond the scope of the program descriptions, including monies paid for additional accommodations or meals.
