Palette

:root {
  --color-primary:       #6089A9;
  --color-primary-dark:  #35596D;
  --color-secondary:     #869B92;

  --color-background:    #FDFDFC;
  --color-background-alt:#B0C4D4;
  --color-warm:          #DADBC5;

  --color-text:          #2D373C;

  --color-accent-gold:   #C1AA5B;
  --color-accent-rust:   #A65A38;
}

How I’d deploy it

Main page background: #FDFDFC
This keeps the site airy and lets the artwork carry visual complexity.

Body text: #2D373C
Much softer and more harmonious than pure black.

Primary headings: #35596D
It relates strongly to the darker blues in the painting while remaining highly legible.

Primary CTA / links: #6089A9
This is very close to the blue used in the large “O” and ICS logo, so it feels particularly native to the source design.

Large coloured sections: #B0C4D4
This is essentially the dominant blue field on the right side of the poster.

Secondary sections: #DADBC5
Useful for alternating backgrounds and introducing some of the warmth of the painting.

Secondary text / eyebrows / tags: #668583 or #869B92
These pick up the green-gray typography and painted tones.

Tiny accents only: #C1AA5B and #A65A38
Use these for things like rules, icons, hover flourishes, dates, or small badges. I would not make either one a major brand colour. Their effectiveness comes from being scarce.
